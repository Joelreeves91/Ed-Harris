Easy is a state, not a pace. Easy running is defined by what your body is doing (aerobic, conversational, low lactate), not by what your watch says. On a warm or tired day, easy might be 7:00/km. On a cool, rested morning it might be 6:15/km. Both are correct if HR stays in Zone 2 (135–148). The pace shown for easy sessions is what that zone typically produces right now, not a target. As the block progresses, that pace drops naturally at the same HR.

Why no VO2max intervals? A half marathon is run just under threshold, and Ed's 5K already outruns his 10K and half by a wide margin. Speed is not the limiter, endurance is. Threshold work, race-pace running and long runs close that gap. Strides and hill sprints keep the neuromuscular sharpness without the fatigue cost of intervals.

Tuesday · full body, heavy legs. Three full-body sessions a week. Tuesday is the only strength day with no hard run in the 48 hours after it, so it carries all the heavy leg work. Friday sits before the long run and Sunday before the quality session, so their leg work stays light and nothing goes near failure. Weeks 1–4: learn the lifts at moderate loads, 2–3 reps in reserve. Weeks 6–16: full sets, trap bar heavy at 4–6 reps. Deloads and weeks 17–21: maintenance, 2 sets at the same loads (muscle and strength hold on far less volume while running peaks). No lifting race week. Anything that reproduces knee or back pain above 3/10: use the regression.

Before Week 1: Health Flags

Current knee and back pain, no physio. If either hurts during running, changes how you run, or is there at rest, get a physio assessment before starting. The plan is built conservatively around it, but it cannot replace an assessment.

Sleep of 5–6 hours is the single biggest limit on how much training you can absorb. Every extra 30 minutes is worth more than any session in this plan.

Weekly Template

  • Monday: quality run. 48 hours after the long run.
  • Tuesday: Strength A, the only heavy leg day. Track Tuesday can replace Monday's quality session, never both in one week: then Monday is free and Strength A follows the track.
  • Wednesday: easy run, in the morning. Marlow Run Club counts if HR stays in Zone 2 and the distance is covered: add the remainder as an easy jog before or after.
  • Thursday: non-negotiable rest.
  • Friday: Strength B, legs light.
  • Saturday: long run. On weekends with your daughter, run it early.
  • Sunday: Strength C, legs light. After 5pm on weekends with your daughter.

What This Block Must Deliver

  • Volume: 15km/week now to a 39km peak (week 19) on three runs.
  • Long run: 10km now to 19km, at least three runs of 16km+.
  • Race-pace endurance: 8km continuous at race pace (week 19) and 6km in the rehearsal (week 20).
  • Threshold ceiling: 5:02/km to 4:48/km, reset by the time trial.
  • Gut training: gels rehearsed on every long run over 75 minutes from week 7.
  • Robustness: strength three times a week, knee and back pain settled or managed.

Pain Rules (knee, back, hamstring)

Green: stiff at the start, gone within 10 minutes, fine the next day. Carry on and monitor.

Amber: pain during the run above 3/10, or still there the next morning, or not improving after 5–7 days. Cut volume 40–60%, no quality or hills, reassess after 5 days. Not improving: physio.

Red: sharp pain, limping, swelling, pain at rest, or the knee giving way. Stop running and get assessed before returning.

Daily Flags

  • RHR 5+ bpm above baseline, 2 consecutive mornings: drop to easy. No quality.
  • RHR 8+ bpm above baseline: full rest. Reassess next morning.
  • Under 5 hours sleep: downgrade quality to easy, skip that day's lifting.
  • Weekend with your daughter and Saturday not possible: move the long run to Sunday evening and skip Strength C. Monday becomes easy, quality moves to Tuesday.
  • Low motivation plus aches 3+ consecutive days: unplanned deload.

Session Signals

  • Easy pace slowing 15+ sec/km at the same HR over a week: fatigue building. Drop the next quality session.
  • Cannot hit T-pace: slow 8 sec/km and call it sub-threshold. Three poor quality sessions in a row: deload.
  • Race pace feels like threshold twice in a row: drop race pace one row in the time trial table.
  • Week 18 or 19 long run falls apart: race for Goal B (1:49).

Missed Sessions

Do not stack missed sessions. The next session is whatever was already planned, not the missed one bolted on. Missed easy run: skip it. Missed quality: move it one day or skip. Missed long run: move it 1–2 days, or shorten by a quarter if 4+ days late.

Exception: the 10K time trial is worth moving rather than skipping. Run it the following Saturday in place of that week’s long run; nothing else moves.

Race Week Illness

Mild cold, head only, no fever: race goes ahead.

Chest, lungs, or fever: do not race. Hard racing on a body fighting infection risks myocarditis. There will be other halves.

Stomach bug 48+ hours before: if fluids have stayed down for 24 hours before the race, start. Otherwise do not.
